# Container geometry + inverted water-sheet washing

**Design note — 2026-09-30**

This records an AllDone design hypothesis for the reusable container and its washing system. It is a project design disclosure, not a claim that prior art has been ruled out.

## Container geometry

The container interior should be one continuous, accessible surface:

- wall-to-base transitions formed as generous circular arcs rather than sharp corners;
- no internal ledges, recesses or geometry where food can hide;
- shaped so a spoon or spatula can reach essentially everything inside;
- the same geometry should favour both complete food removal by the user and predictable wash flow.

The user-facing goal is simple: more of the food should actually be consumed rather than left inaccessible in corners. That also reduces the residue presented to the wash system.

## Inverted two-pass water-sheet hypothesis

Wash the container upside down.

Instead of treating a single round jet as the default, use a very thin, high-velocity **sheet of water** launched exactly alongside an interior wall.

The target path is:

1. **A → B:** the first sheet climbs wall A, follows the rounded wall/base transition, crosses the container base and continues toward/down opposite wall B.
2. **C → D:** a second, perpendicular sheet does the same across the other axis.

These are sequential passes, not opposing streams fired into each other.

The intended effect is closer to a liquid blade than a conventional spray. At the moving front, the sheet should break adhesion and push residue away from the glass; the following high-speed film supplies shear and carries the detached material with it. Because the container is inverted, gravity then helps the loosened food and water leave the open mouth instead of collecting elsewhere inside the vessel.

The design target is therefore not maximum pressure or maximum water. It is **maximum useful detachment, shear and evacuation per millilitre**.

## Water-only recovery stage

The first A→B and C→D passes are water only.

Their runoff is collected separately, before detergent or sanitizer enters the process, because this first fraction should contain most of whatever food remained after normal emptying. The recovered organic fraction can then be dewatered and directed toward the food-to-soil stream rather than mixed immediately into general wash water.

The sequence to test is:

**empty/invert → A→B water sheet → collect → C→D water sheet → collect → hygienic wash/sanitation only if still required**

The working hypothesis is deliberately aggressive: with the right container radius, sheet thickness, velocity and pulse duration, those two perpendicular water-only passes may remove essentially all gross food residue. Anything after that should be treated as hygiene/sanitation rather than as the primary means of removing food.

## What to measure

The experiment should vary:

- sheet/slot thickness;
- water velocity and pressure;
- pulse duration;
- wall/base radius;
- base profile;
- container dimensions;
- water temperature;
- residue type and drying time.

Measure:

- water used per container;
- remaining visible and measurable food residue;
- recovered organic mass;
- redeposition;
- time per container;
- detergent needed after the water-only passes;
- sanitation result after the complete cycle.

Test against difficult realistic residues, including starch, fat, protein, sugar/sauce and fibrous material.

The success criterion is not merely that the vessel looks cleaner. The aim is to find the minimum-resource process that reaches a defined refill-safe cleanliness threshold while recovering as much residual food as practical before detergent enters the stream.

## Ownership consequence

The container should be good enough that households can reasonably own their container allocation rather than treating it as disposable packaging. Ownership can create a direct incentive not to lose or break containers. Operationally, the physical containers can still circulate as a pooled interchangeable fleet; household ownership need not mean receiving the exact same serial-numbered container back.

The design therefore has to work simultaneously as something pleasant to use, easy to empty completely, difficult to damage, highly stackable and exceptionally efficient to clean.
